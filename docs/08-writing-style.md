# 08. Writing that does not read as AI

Recruiters and founders now skim for signs that a message was generated. These rules apply to resumes, form answers, emails, DMs and follow-ups.

## Banned characters

- No **em-dashes**, **en-dashes** or **middle dots**. They are the most common giveaway.
- Use a comma, a period, a plain hyphen (`-`), parentheses, or `|` in signatures and resume headers.
- Restructure the sentence rather than swap the character: "I built X, which cut Y" instead of joining two clauses with a long dash.
- `fact-check.py` and `preflight.sh` flag all three. Run them on drafts too, not only resumes.

## Short and specific

- Say the concrete thing: "a FastAPI webhook service for 6 carrier integrations", not "robust, scalable backend solutions".
- One idea per sentence. Most sentences under 20 words.
- Real names of tools, real numbers from the factsheet, real company and product names (theirs, never your employer's confidential ones).
- Cut the first sentence if it is a warm-up.
- Emails 60 to 90 words. Form answers usually 2 to 6 sentences. LinkedIn notes under 300 characters.

## Words and patterns to avoid

- Buzzwords: leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, passionate, spearheaded, dynamic, robust, seamless, innovative, game-changing, delve, tapestry, landscape, "in today's fast-paced world".
- Filler openers: "I hope this email finds you well", "I am writing to express my interest", "I came across your profile and was impressed".
- Triple lists of adjectives. Rhetorical questions. Exclamation marks.
- "Not only X but also Y", "It's not just X, it's Y".
- Closing with three options or "Looking forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience".
- Bold or headers inside emails.

## Sound like a person

- First person, contractions are fine ("I'm", "I've").
- Match the other person's tone and length in replies. Short casual message in, short casual reply out.
- A P.S. that is true and specific to them.
- It is fine to be plain. Plain and specific reads as human. Polished and generic reads as a bot.

## Honest framing

- Say what you did, not what the team did: "I built", "I fixed", "I wrote". If it was shared, "I worked with".
- If you have not used something, say so and name the closest thing you have done.
- No invented numbers to make a sentence sound stronger.

## Quick self-check before sending

1. Any em-dash, en-dash or middle dot? Remove.
2. Does line 1 mention them, not me?
3. Is every claim in the factsheet?
4. Could this sentence appear in anyone's email? Make it specific or cut it.
5. Is there exactly one question for them to answer?
